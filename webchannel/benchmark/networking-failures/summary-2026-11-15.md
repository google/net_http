# iOS networking-failure results: summary and next steps (2026-11-15)

This summarizes the runs of 2026-11-12 to 2026-11-14 (about 75 trials on a
physical iPhone against the hosted staging endpoint, SDK unchanged). It covers what
they confirm, where the [test spec](../../specs/ios-networking-tests.md) was wrong,
and what comes next. Details and raw evidence are in the dated reports.

## Confirmed

- **Timers are exact:**
  - back-channel GET timeout 45.00 s after the last chunk;
  - handshake timeout 45 s, never retried;
  - POST timeout 10–20 s;
  - keep-alive 30 s.
- **Idle blackhole:**
  - 10 s: no effect.
  - 50 s: same-session recovery.
  - 90 s: 2 of 3 recover, 1 gets 400 (UnknownSessionID).
  - 150 s: always 400.
- **Pending write in a blackhole:** abort at 40–55 s. With `failFast`: abort at
  13–18 s, with no forward retry.
- **Network off:** a 60 s outage aborts at 18–41 s; with a pending write, 9–16 s.
- **Handoffs and streaming:**
  - Streaming and handoffs show no message gaps or duplicates.
  - Wi-Fi → cellular recovers in 0.6–2.4 s.
  - Wi-Fi → blackholed cellular resumes within 1–2 s of Wi-Fi returning, for outages
    up to about 108 s.
- **Protocol:** every request used HTTP/2. The staging endpoint never led URLSession
  to HTTP/3, so **HTTP/3 behavior is not covered yet**. The test spec now has an
  [HTTP/3 variant (§9)](../../specs/ios-networking-tests.md#9-http3-variant); see Next
  steps.

## Corrections to the expected behavior

| Topic | Expected | Observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long idle blackhole | Abort at about 165–225 s | 167–187 s. The first retry reuses the dead HTTP/2 connection and fails in about 18–21 s, not 45 s. When iOS marks the path unsatisfied during the loss (about 85–100 s in), the remaining retries fail at once and the abort comes at about 90–120 s. |
| Periodic sends (M5) | Reset the forward retry count; the back channel bounds the abort | Abort at 51–82 s. The count is reset only when no forward retry is already scheduled, which is rarely the case during a blackhole. |
| Back-channel rotation (M1) | 45–60 s | 60.0–60.2 s: the server closes the GET at the first noop after its 45 s refresh interval |
| Session lifetime | 60–120 s after the loss | About 120 s after the **last GET start** (60 s rotation + 60 s). Resumes at 68 s worked; at 128 s or more they got 400. |
| Wi-Fi → cellular | `-1005`, then a retry at attempt 2 | No status-0 failure: the cut-off GET is treated as a normal end, and a new GET starts at attempt 1. Fast, but the error is invisible to the app. |
| Suspension (T15) | `-1005` on resume | No `-1005`. iOS runs the app about 32 s in the background first. On resume, the GET either completes with buffered data or its overdue timeout fires at once. |

## Gaps observed in the current client

1. **Slow recovery after the network returns:** 17–25 s after a 10 s outage (T9). The
   client waits for its next scheduled retry instead of reacting to the network
   coming back.
2. **Flapping is fatal:** 2 of 3 channels failed after three short Wi-Fi cuts (T11),
   because each cut uses up retries.
3. **The give-up time exceeds the server session window:** about 170–190 s of retries,
   but a resume only works within about 120 s of the last GET start.
4. **Errors are invisible:** the underlying `NSError` is never surfaced, and a GET cut
   off after a 200 doesn't count as a failure.
5. **Interface choice lags the path callback:** for about 1 s after Wi-Fi returns, new
   connections may still use cellular.

## Next steps

**Planned client improvements:**
- **L3-aware recovery:** pause retries while the network is down, and resume at once
   (counts reset) when it comes back. This addresses gaps 1 and 2 and the "path
   unsatisfied" early aborts.
- **Error detail:** surface the underlying transport error, and, with `failFast`,
   treat a response cut off after a 200 as a failure (gap 4).
- **Recovery windows:** tune the retry counts, intervals and server keep-alive (e.g.
   20 s) so the client's give-up time fits the server session window (gap 3).

**HTTP/3 coverage (required), per the
[HTTP/3 variant (§9) of the test spec](../../specs/ios-networking-tests.md#9-http3-variant):**
all results so far are HTTP/2. HTTP/3 (QUIC) can
behave differently in exactly the cases tested here: dead-connection detection,
retry timing, Wi-Fi ↔ cellular (QUIC connection migration), and suspension (QUIC
idle timeout).
- **Get h3:**
  - The hosted staging host supports HTTP/3. Check whether URLSession learns it on the
    test network: log the `Alt-Svc` response header and check for an HTTPS DNS record.
  - If it doesn't, set `URLRequest.assumesHTTP3Capable` (iOS 14.5+) on the WebChannel
    requests through an opt-in demo/SDK hook.
  - Accept a run only when `networkProtocolName == "h3"` for the WebChannel requests.
- **Re-run on h3 and compare with the h2 results:**
  - T2–T5: how fast iOS fails a retry on a dead QUIC connection, and the abort timing.
  - T7, and T9/T11.
  - T12–T14: does the connection migrate, and does it keep the same connection?
  - T15.
  - M6: the QUIC `max_idle_timeout` iOS advertises, and its PING cadence, from a packet
    capture or qlog.

**Additional tests:**
- **Network path:** repeat the long blackhole (T5) with the current wiring (no Linux
  box in the path) to see whether the 18–21 s first-retry failure and the "path
  unsatisfied" aborts depend on it.
- **Expiry threshold:** add a suspension run at about 45–60 s to locate the
  roughly 120 s-from-last-GET boundary.
- **Before/after:** after the client changes land, re-run T5, T9, T11 and T15 against
  this baseline.
- **Error codes:** capture the underlying error codes once they're exposed, to
  confirm `-1005`/`-1009`.
